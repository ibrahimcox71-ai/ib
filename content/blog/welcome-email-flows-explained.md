A welcome email flow is an automated series of emails sent to new subscribers in their
first days on your list. It matters because new subscribers are at peak interest: they
just chose to hear from you. A good welcome flow introduces your brand, sets expectations,
and guides subscribers toward a first purchase while that interest is still warm.

## What exactly is a welcome email flow?

A welcome email flow (also called a welcome series or onboarding sequence) is a set of
pre-written emails that send automatically when someone joins your list. In Klaviyo or
Mailchimp you define the trigger ("joins list") once, then each email sends on a schedule —
for example immediately, after two days, and after four days — without you touching anything.

## Why does it matter for a small store?

- **Timing beats volume.** The moment someone subscribes is the moment they care most.
  A flow reaches them then — every time, even at 3 a.m.
- **It runs on autopilot.** You write the series once; it works for every future subscriber.
- **It does a newsletter's job better.** Newsletters hit everyone at once; a welcome flow
  hits each person at the right moment for *them*.

## What should a welcome flow contain?

A practical 3–5 email structure I use as a starting point:

1. **Email 1 (immediate):** thank them, deliver whatever was promised (discount, guide),
   and say what kind of emails to expect.
2. **Email 2 (+2 days):** the brand story — why you exist, what makes you different,
   plus best-sellers as social proof.
3. **Email 3 (+4 days):** handle the top objection (shipping, returns, quality) and
   invite a reply — replies teach you about your customers.
4. **Optional Email 4 (+7 days):** a gentle urgency nudge if a welcome offer is expiring.

## How long should each email be?

Shorter than you think. One idea per email, one clear button. Readers skim, especially on
phones — a welcome email that tries to say five things converts like it said zero.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Sending only one welcome email and stopping.
- Leading with "10% off" before the subscriber knows who you are.
- Writing five different calls to action in one email.
- Never revisiting the flow — review it after a few months and A/B test subject lines
  and timing, one variable at a time.

## How do I get one?

If you already use Klaviyo or Mailchimp, the building blocks exist in your account; the work
is the strategy, the copy and the setup. If you'd rather hand it off — a welcome flow is one
of the first things I build for clients, alongside abandoned cart flows and segmentation.
