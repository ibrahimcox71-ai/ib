Klaviyo is usually the stronger choice for e-commerce brands that want deep store data,
granular segments and behavior-based flows. Mailchimp suits smaller lists, simpler
newsletters and tighter starting budgets. Neither is "best" for everyone — the right pick
depends on your store size, your platform and how much you plan to automate.

## How do they differ, at a glance?

| Question | Klaviyo | Mailchimp |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | E-commerce stores that live on customer data | Small lists, newsletters, simpler needs |
| Store data | Deep sync: products, orders, browsing events | Basic sync via integrations |
| Segmentation | Very granular behavioral segments | Simpler, tag/audience based |
| Flows/automation | Powerful branching logic | Capable, but shallower for complex flows |
| Starting cost | Competitive entry tier; scales with list size | Familiar free/cheap entry for tiny lists |

Pricing and features change regularly — always check both sites for current plan details
before deciding. The table is about *philosophy*, which changes slower than price tags.

## When should you choose Klaviyo?

Choose Klaviyo when email is (or should become) a serious revenue channel:

- You run on Shopify, WooCommerce or similar and want emails based on **what people
  actually did** — viewed a product, started checkout, bought twice last quarter.
- You want **flows with branches** — different messages for different behaviors, not
  one linear sequence.
- You want **A/B testing inside flows**, so the automation itself improves over time.

## When is Mailchimp enough?

Mailchimp is a reasonable choice when:

- Your list is small and you mostly send **monthly newsletters**.
- You need a familiar interface and quick setup more than deep automation.
- Budget at the starting tier is the deciding factor.

Many brands start on Mailchimp and migrate when flows and segmentation become the
bottleneck — that's a normal growth path, not a failure.

## What about migrating between them?

Migrating is very doable: export contacts with their tags/fields, rebuild signup forms,
then recreate flows. The real cost isn't moving the data — it's rebuilding the *logic*
(segments and flow branches). Plan a quiet week for it and keep both tools running in
parallel briefly so no flow email is missed.

## Which one do I recommend after working in both?

For jewelry, fashion and similar considered-purchase stores I've worked with — Klaviyo,
because segmentation and behavior-based flows are exactly where revenue hides. For a
local service business sending a monthly update, Mailchimp's simplicity wins. If you're
